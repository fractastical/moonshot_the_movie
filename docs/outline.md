# MOONSHOT — cut outline

Working document for the Future Vision XPRIZE submission. Deadline **15 August 2026**;
deliverable is a 3:00 trailer at 1080p or better, plus a treatment and cover sheet.

Starting point is `~/Downloads/Contest video long version 2.mp4` — a finished 2:03 cut at
1920×1080, 30fps, 27 Mbps. This document maps that cut second by second, then specifies
the 57 seconds that turn it into a three-minute film with a story in it.

### Three findings

**There is no antagonist anywhere in 123 seconds.** Nothing pushes back at any point,
which is why the cut reads as a mood reel rather than a story. This is the whole problem
and it is also, conveniently, the thing that fills the gap to 3:00.

**The music has one dynamic level for 110 of 123 seconds.** Quiet intro to 0:07, hard drop
at 0:08, then RMS pinned at 0.42 until a fade at 1:57. The arrangement does change — the
bass share of the spectrum steps down at 0:38 and again at 1:35 — but everything is
mastered to the same loudness, so none of it can be felt. There is no silence anywhere in
the film, so the script's "music cuts to silence" has nowhere to land.

**The picture breathes but the score ignores it.** The edit holds long at 0:20–0:29 on the
surveillance wall and again at 1:34–1:47 on the lunar material, while the track plays at
full party volume over both. That mismatch is what makes the music feel applied rather
than intrinsic.

## 2. The story the insert has to tell

**One currency: energy.** The quota, the breaker, the crowd, the 1969 expenditure — the
film is denominated end to end in the same thing, and the argument is over who is
allowed to spend it. Nothing below works unless that reads.

**The moon has been filed as finished, and that is the antagonist's entire case.** We
went, we planted a flag, the item was closed. A completed world needs no power, so
consumption is capped at what a completed world costs. This is not a safety measure, it
is an audit against a closed ledger, and it is why he is warm rather than frightening:
he is not preventing a danger, he is declining to fund a redundancy. *"We used to look
up at the same thing. Once"* is not wistful. It is the exhibit.

**The ritual has a cause, and the cause is the invoice.** The scientists lose their
allocation, cannot run a laboratory on nothing, and build the apparatus out of people
instead — light, sound, and a roomful of attention pointed the same way. Everything in
Act II is a substitution for equipment they can no longer power. The circle is not a
séance, it is a transmitter, and the beams leave the building.

**The venue is a tabernacle, and its materials are part of the argument.** Not a
warehouse — a temporary sanctuary of raw pine framing and silver mylar sheeting,
faceted into an octagonal passage and chamber and erected inside a larger dark hall.
Cushions in a ring, candles at the centre, crossed beams overhead. It is a cathedral
built out of what a defunded lab can still afford from a builders' merchant, and it is
portable in the oldest sense of the word: a structure you put up because you are
expecting something to arrive. Every generated party shot has to be in this building,
which is why it now has its own reference plate.

**Which makes the number evidence, not a score.** It reads signal strength, and it is the
only proof that an unmetered supply exists. That is why he cannot simply outvote it.

**And it is why cutting the power fails.** He models them as consumers of electricity;
they are not, which is the whole point of the ritual. *"They kept dancing. In the dark.
Which we had not modelled"* is his accounting framework failing to parse what it is
looking at.

**The antagonist is an erasure, not a police force.** The Softening does not ban the
party — it removes the evidence that the party happened. Its position is sympathetic and
widely held, which is what makes it credible where clowns were not.

**Its escalation is one gesture at four scales.** Turn down the speakers. Turn down the
lights. Turn down the meter. Turn down the moon — not destroy it, *dim* it, because a
closed file that stays visible keeps getting reopened. That last one is his operation
name, seen on a document at 1:42. The film's title is the other side's name for the same
event, and the two of them compete for the last card.

**Three parties, not two.** The Nothing, which feeds on agreement. The thing the crowd is
assembling, which is never named because you count it instead. And the instrument — the
chrome in the corner of every frame, which reads for four acts, declines to corroborate
him at 1:49, and signs on at 2:46. It never speaks. A signature is not an argument.

**It never has to attack the meter directly at first.** Vibe is motion energy, so capping
decibels and power genuinely makes the crowd move less and the number genuinely falls.
The Softening throttles the inputs and the readout dies on its own — which is far more
sinister than deletion, because it looks like the party's own verdict on itself. They can
cite your data back at you, warmly, and be right.

**The reversal:** the crowd adapts, dances harder in the dark, and the number climbs
anyway. Only then does the meter have to go — not because it is dangerous, but because it
stopped agreeing with them.

**The stakes are connection, not measurement.** When the meter dies, the geomap goes dark
and the rooms can no longer see each other. Each one has to wonder whether it is the last
one still dancing. That is the loss the audience feels; a deleted dashboard is not.

**The turn:** you cannot defeat an absence, you fill it. One person dances inside the
silence and the counter ticks up from zero. One subject. Then two.

**Guard rail.** Do not let this read as pro-surveillance. The party built its own meter and
opted into it; the Softening deletes *your* telemetry and keeps its own. One line of
dialogue fixes this and it must be in there.

## 3. Proposed 3:00 structure

Nothing in the existing cut is locked, so this is a restructure rather than an insert.

The reason to restructure rather than splice: **the film currently has no setup.** It drops
at 0:08 and never stops. Adding an antagonist to a film with no setup produces a film with
an antagonist and no setup — the Softening's threat only lands if the audience already
understands that the meter matters, and right now the meter is one image among hundreds
flying past at two-thirds of a second each. Acts I and II exist to make the meter matter.

The other reason: a film where every shot is the same length has tempo but no rhythm.
Shot length should differentiate by act — patient in I, fast in III, deliberately slow in
IV — and right now the whole thing runs at a uniform 0.66s.

| Act | Time | Length | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **I — The flatline** | 0:00–0:35 | 35s | The crisis. Not a party yet. Van arriving, the automated world, nobody outside, the index at zero |
| **II — The ritual** | 0:35–1:05 | 30s | The circle. Candles, beams, headbands. The number wakes up |
| **III — The signal spreads** | 1:05–1:35 | 30s | Scientists, beasts, swarm, real party. The map lights up. The number climbs |
| **IV — The Softening** | 1:35–2:25 | 50s | The antagonist act |
| **V — The answer** | 2:25–3:00 | 35s | Moon eye, lunar room, moon dance, van portrait |

Shot lengths by act: **I** 3–5s, **II** 4–6s, **III** 0.5–1s, **IV** 2–3s, **V** 2–4s.

### What changes from the existing cut

**Act I grows from 7s to 35s.** This is the biggest single change. Your premise is a global
celebration index of 0.03% and that fact is currently nowhere on screen. Build it from the
van arrival, the empty B&W surveillance wall with nobody in frame, a vibe readout sitting
at 3, and stock #65 — a room full of CRTs still showing the 1969 landing with nobody
watching. The old signal, still transmitting, unattended. That single shot establishes the
whole thesis before a word is spoken.

**The ritual grows from 6s to 30s.** It is the most credible and most beautiful footage in
the film — the thing that looks like it actually happened — and it currently gets six
seconds including a solarised repeat. This is where the biosensor headbands read, and
where the number should visibly wake up. *Dependency: we only have what survives in the
contest cut. Giving this act 30 seconds needs the original ZVEN source.*

**Roughly 20 seconds of montage comes out.** The stretch at 0:54–1:11 — the burning
building, the mech figure, the abstract magenta washes — does no narrative work and is the
weakest material in the film. The choppiest part of 1:12–1:33 can also thin considerably.

**The `BLOOD MOON IMMINENT` card goes.** It belongs to the old werewolf antagonist and
means nothing once the Softening is the opposition. If a card is wanted in that slot, it
should be a compliance notice.

**The van bookend survives at both ends**, untouched.

## 4. Sourcing every new shot

**Already in hand.**

- CRT wall dying: `Moon and Astronauts/Moon Walking Landing/old-television-sets-display-1969-moon-landing…mov` (17s). A dark room of vintage CRTs all showing the 1969 landing. This is the film's thesis in one image — the moon was the original shared signal — and killing the screens one at a time is the erasure beat.
- Network retracting: `bioart_vj_loop_pack/mycelium_pulse_8s_loop.mp4` **reversed**.
- Figures corrupting: `Astronauts/futuristic-astronauts-walking-in-glitch-space-scene…mp4` (5s).
- The live instrument: `bioart_vj_loop_pack/biota_record_8s_loop.mp4` — a circular dish of clustered points, the meter's biological twin; kill it alongside the geomap.
- Patience shot if needed: `Moon Loops/an-airliner-lines-up-for-a-nighttime-final-approac…mov` (49s) — an airliner crossing a huge golden moon.
- Ritual punctuation: `VAJRA DORJE ANIMATIONS/DORJE 3D ROTATING 1.mp4`.
- Moonshot rhyme, if the resolution needs lifting: `Astronauts/futuristic-astronauts-walk-through-neon-ring-tunne…mov` — concentric orange rings that rhyme with both the sacred geometry projected on the ritual floor and the op-art spiral on the HYPERSTITION poster.

**Renderable from our own system** — these are the strongest shots in the insert and none
of them need generating:

- Vibe readout falling, then climbing. Real telemetry, real UI.
- Detection boxes emptying; subject count running backwards.
- Geomap pins going dark one by one.
- Compliance overlays in the existing chrome.

**The antagonist already exists.** `~/Downloads/smug_nazi_looking_guy_in_a_bla.mp4`, 10s,
720p, and it was sitting unused. A man in a dark coat in an institutional corridor,
already monochrome, **holding DEFANGED NOUMENA up to camera** — the paperback this
document specced as a prop. Smug, pleased with himself, walking away down the corridor.

He is not the wellness inspector and should not be cut as one. He is the man who *wrote*
the argument: the author of *Selected Wellness Writings*, who never attends the party he
is closing and never touches a sound meter. That is a better structure than the original
plan, because it separates the ideologue from the field staff and gives the warm V.O. an
owner. The corridor being nothing like the tabernacle is the point.

Cut into Act IV at three places, using the whole clip: his face at the peak (1:35.6, vibe
75, the antagonist appears while the party is at its height), the book held to camera as
the case is made (1:42.6), and at 2:03.8 he **salutes the room he has just killed and
walks out** — a bent-arm temple salute, contemptuous, before the erasure has even
finished, because it is already done.

**On how evil he should be.** Very — but the register matters, and it is a craft point
rather than a squeamish one. This whole antagonist exists because "clown riot police" had
no argumentative force. The Softening works because the audience half-agrees with it: you
turned joy into a KPI, you are scoring your own ecstasy, the number is the disease. A
villain you can dismiss on sight cannot make that argument, and beating him means nothing.
So the note is not "less evil", it is: **smug, not jackbooted.** Cold certainty, amusement,
a man so sure he is right that he finds the whole thing funny. The salute is exactly that
and it stays in. Anything that tips him into costume — insignia, a straight-arm salute,
uniformed muscle — defuses him and should be refused on those grounds.

**Still needs generating.** Down from six shots to four.

1. **Wellness inspectors arriving.** The field staff. Three or four people in soft sage-green softshell with lanyards and handheld sound meters, walking calmly through a low timber doorway into the hazy candlelit tabernacle at night. One offers a bottle of water to a dancer. Unhurried, kind, no uniforms, no weapons. Handheld, available light, **monochrome or near** — see section 7.
2. **The aftermath — ritual circle.** The same tabernacle as the ZVEN footage — raw pine framing hung with silver mylar, erected inside a larger dark hall — candles extinguished and smoking, meditation cushions stacked in a neat pile, house lights up, nobody there. Static, wide.
3. **Dancing in the dark.** A crowd in near-total darkness, lit only by a phone or two, still dancing hard. High grain, almost monochrome.
4. **One dancer in the silence.** A single figure dancing alone in an empty lit room, seen from behind, unselfconscious.

The sound-meter insert and the empty-dancefloor shot both came out of the list: the first
is covered by the author's own footage plus a rendered readout, the second is redundant
with the ritual aftermath. The aftermath shot remains the highest value per second and the
easiest to generate, because nothing moves in it.

## 5. The score — built

Done. `build/video/score.wav`, 3:00, 128 BPM, A minor.

    .venv/bin/python3 tools/video/curve.py    # assemble the automation, plot it
    .venv/bin/python3 tools/video/score.py    # render the audio, plot data vs. loudness

The music is not composed to picture. Every parameter that matters — filter cutoff, which
layers are playing, whether the drums enter, whether the hook states at all — is driven by
an automation curve made of vibe recorded during the exhibition. Nothing in the shape of
this track was drawn by hand.

**Where the curve comes from.** One continuous window couldn't serve the film, because the
strongest single stretch in the archive peaks at its end and Act IV needs a collapse. So
`curve.py` searches all sessions for a real window matching each act's required shape,
allowing time-scaling between 0.7× and 1.45×, and refuses to let two acts quote the same
minute of the same night. The seams land on act boundaries, which are picture cuts.

| Act | Source session | From | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| I flatline | 22 Jul | t=24998s | 5–7 |
| II ritual | 21 Jul | t=13114s | 11–28 |
| III spread | 20 Jul | t=182s | 51–80 |
| IV softening | 25 Jul | t=19840s | 77 → 0 |
| V answer | 20 Jul | t=328s | 80–87 |

Act IV is the one that matters and it is a single unbroken 49 seconds from the night of 25
July: a room at 77 that sags, spikes back to 72 in defiance, and then goes to absolute zero
and stays there. The search was constrained to reject any candidate that merely dipped —
the act needed a real erasure, and the archive had one.

**What the number does.** Bass enters around 10, kick around 16, snare and hats around 32,
the hook around 42, and the octave-up doubling only above 70. The cutoff sweeps 240 Hz to
5.8 kHz. The three section channels drive three pad voices panned left, centre and right,
so when the room's thirds drift apart the chord spreads, which is counterpoint for free.
Underneath it all is a bed of mains hum, CRT whine and tape hiss whose gain is the
*inverse* of the vibe: it opens the film, the party buries it, and it comes back when the
Softening empties the room. The hum returning is the antagonist winning.

**Measured dynamics**, the thing the current cut lacks:

| | I | II | III | IV | V |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RMS dBFS | −22.0 | −14.3 | −10.5 | −13.8 | −10.5 |

Inside Act IV, the Nothing sits at −27.7 and the turn slams back to −10.5: **a 17.2 dB
jump on one cut**, and it is a genuine cut, because the IV→V boundary is the one act
boundary where the automation is deliberately left unsmoothed. The number holds absolute
zero until 2:24.0 and is at 80.9 by 2:24.5.

### Corrected after the first viewing

The first pass had the flatline at −28.2 and the ritual at −19.7, which meant the opening
**sixty-five seconds of a three-minute film had no audible music** — the note on the cut
was, fairly, "basically no music". That was an over-correction against the contest track's
flat mastering: the fix for one dynamic level is not two thirds of a film at none.

Three changes. The arrangement thresholds came down across the board — bass now enters at
5 rather than 10, kick at 9 rather than 16, hats at 22 rather than 32, the hook at 30
rather than 42 — because the old thresholds left most of the film sparse regardless of
gain. The low end of the master curve came up. And a **surveillance layer** was added: a
sub heartbeat and a struck tone that exist only while the room is nearly empty and recede
as the party takes over, so the flatline has actual music rather than a filtered drone.
Like the room tone, it is gated to vanish at a true zero, so the Nothing is untouched.

The result keeps 11.5 dB between the flatline and the party — felt, but never absent.

## 7. The look — degraded, and driven by the same curve

    .venv/bin/python3 tools/video/looks.py    # candidate treatments + the drain test

Writes `build/video/look_tests.png` (five treatments over three stand-in frames) and
`build/video/look_drain.png` (Act IV graded off its own telemetry).

The GENERATE cards originally specified "photoreal, available light", which is the most
expensive thing to ask a generator for and the hardest to hold consistent across five
shots. Tested against camcorder-1997, monochrome noir, our own surveillance rig, and a
noir/failing-stream hybrid, photoreal is also the *weakest* of the five.

**Monochrome is the answer, and it is not a style choice.** Three reasons, in order of
importance:

1. **It collapses the consistency problem.** Five shots from a generator will not match
   on skin tone, colour temperature or grade. In monochrome under heavy grain, they will.
   This is the difference between a deliverable and a risk.
2. **It is the film's native format.** Our own rig outputs black-and-white surveillance
   plates — that is literally what is in `~/Documents/surveille`, and the contest cut is
   already carrying B&W monitor walls, a B&W filmstrip and a B&W van portrait. Mono is not
   applied over this film, it is what this film is made of.
3. **It fixes the Softening's weakest link.** If the inspectors are only ever seen *through
   the party's own cameras*, then the party's surveillance is the sole witness to its own
   erasure — and when the Softening deletes the footage, it is deleting the only evidence
   they were ever there. Far stronger than seeing them in clean coverage.

Point 3 also converts work from GENERATE to RENDER: a rough generated plate played through
our existing monitor/deskew/detection pipeline comes out as our own footage, chrome and
all. The generator only has to be good enough to survive being degraded.

### The drain

The grade is a function of the vibe curve, exactly as the score's arrangement is. Colour,
contrast, halation and grain all ride the same automation the music reads, so the picture
drains as the Softening throttles the room and snaps back on the turn. Nothing is
keyframed to picture. It is the same numbers.

The important detail is that the Softening does **two** things and they need two different
signatures:

| | Cause | Signature | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Drain** | Throttling the inputs — the room genuinely moves less | Colour and contrast fall away | Anywhere; it is a pure function of vibe |
| **Corrupt** | Deleting the record itself | Blocking, tearing, dropout, scanlines | Only from 1:58, once they go after the meter |

Keeping them apart matters, and testing caught this: tying glitch to low vibe put tear
artifacts on Act I's van arrival, which is wrong. A low number in Act I means an empty
room, not a failing tape. The corruption ramps in over the kill and holds through the
Nothing.

The payoff is visible in `look_drain.png`: at 1:50 the frame is nearly monochrome but
clean, at 1:57 **the colour comes back** as the crowd dances harder and the number climbs
to 70, and from 2:04 it drains again and starts tearing. The hope beat lands visually with
no keyframing at all, because the crowd really did that.

### What this changes in the GENERATE specs

Drop "photoreal". All five shots are now: monochrome or near-monochrome, high grain,
available light, handheld, and framed as if captured by a fixed or bodycam-grade sensor.
Shots 1 and 2 — the inspectors and the sound meter — should be specified as seen through
our monitor wall, which is the cheapest and most thematically loaded option available.

## 8. The field — the picture generated from the same numbers

Three shots are no longer footage we bought or slates we owed. They are a WebGPU particle
life simulation ([kajukabla/3d-life-sim](https://github.com/kajukabla/3d-life-sim), MIT,
vendored unmodified at `vendor/lifesim`) automated off the very telemetry that drives the
score and the grade.

This closes the last gap in the film's argument. Until now the vibe curve moved the music
and moved the colour, but every image was still something we found. The claim that the
crowd generated the film had a hole in it exactly where the pictures were. These three
shots fill it: when the network on screen tightens or lets go, it is doing so because the
room did, on that night, at that second.

**How it is driven.** The sim is not patched. It exposes two hooks — `__lifesimApplyPreset`
and `__lifesimInjectAudioFrame` — and `lifesim.html` uses them to feed it synthetic
analysis frames whose three bands carry vibe instead of sound. Low is the left section,
mid the middle, high the right. The band-to-parameter mapping lives in
`tools/lifesim/make_preset.py` and is the whole translation from crowd to picture:
persistence and diffusion decide whether the network remembers its structure or forgets
it, emission and tempo decide how hard it is living, brightness and bloom decide how
present it is. Deliberately gentle. The base preset runs with quorum sensing, flocking and
energy all at zero — its organism comes out of the base physics — so switching those on
does not intensify the look, it substitutes a different simulation and the swarm fills the
domain until you are filming the walls. The equilibrium is the art.

**What replaced what.**

| At | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| 0:57, 4.1s | stock biota loop | the meter's twin, grown on Act II telemetry (vibe 27) |
| 1:30.6, 5.0s | `RENDER` slate | the map alight, Act III at peak (vibe 77) |
| 2:16, 4.95s | stock mycelium, reversed | the network retracting, Act IV collapse (48 → 14) |

The retraction is the one that matters, and it moved. The original slot at 2:16 sits in the
ten seconds of absolute zero that close Act IV — nothing left to retract, and a dead field
is a still frame. The collapse is the eight seconds *before* the Nothing, where the room
falls from its last surge at 1:58 to almost nothing by 2:06. That fall is the shot, and it
is real.

Each shot gets its own seed, framing and cohort count, because three cuts of an
unvaried field read as the same object filmed three times: tissue in Act II, territory in
Act III, a whole body coming apart in Act IV.

**How they are made.** There is no offline renderer — upstream keeps that private — so we
film it running. `tools/lifesim/render.py` opens Chrome, plays the curve window through the
page, records the canvas, and cuts the exact slot out of the result. Headless Chrome on
macOS has no WebGPU, so a real window appears for the length of the capture.

    .venv/bin/python3 tools/lifesim/export_curve.py   # windows out of the film curve
    .venv/bin/python3 tools/lifesim/render.py         # film them

The same page, pointed at `?src=bus` instead of a curve, is the live exhibition layer: it
listens to the VibeMap control bus and runs off the room in real time. Built once, used
twice. Any footage published from it carries the upstream MIT attribution, including the
Fluoddity and AgX credits it inherits — see `vendor/lifesim/PROVENANCE.md`.

## 9. Cost of the restructure

This is a re-edit, not a re-shoot. Almost all of it is existing footage in different
proportions, plus six generated shots and a set of renders from our own system. Twelve
days is enough for that. Two things could break the schedule and both are worth resolving
early:

- **The ritual act needs the original ZVEN source.** *One point nine seconds* survive in
  the contest cut — see section 6 — and the act wants thirty. Without the source, Act II
  has to be rebuilt around the aftermath shots and the biosensor inserts instead, which is
  weaker but survivable. This is now the most urgent question on the list.
- **Five generated shots at a consistent look.** Largely defused by section 7: monochrome
  under heavy grain hides the mismatch between generator outputs, and routing two of the
  five through our own monitor pipeline removes them from the generator's hands almost
  entirely. If it still doesn't hold, cut to the two aftermath shots, which carry the most
  meaning and are the easiest to generate because nothing moves in them.

If either slips, the fallback is the original plan: leave Acts I–III as they are, insert
the Softening at 1:33, and accept that the film has no setup. Worse, but shippable.

## 10. The restructure — built

The animatic described above had a structural fault that only became visible once it
existed: **the film ran half its length before it had a conflict.** The Softening first
appeared at 1:35. Acts I, II and III — ninety-five seconds — contained no antagonist at
all, which meant Act I read as a world that was inexplicably dead rather than one that
had been made dead. The whole argument of the film is that the quiet is *enforced*, and
that argument arrived far too late to organise anything before it.

Three changes, all inside the existing 180 seconds, all act totals unchanged.

**The conflict is now stated at 0:10.** A new red card, *YOU HAVE USED TOO MUCH POWER* —
an automated notice capping a building's draw, nobody in shot. One sentence carries the
whole thesis, in words nobody has to be told twice, and the second line names the reason:
the new limit is what a finished world needs. It buys its 4.5 seconds by trimming the van
arrival, the 1969 landing and the airliner by two seconds each.

**Its author is glimpsed at 0:24,** for 2.5 seconds, unexplained — footage we already
hold, walking away down an institutional corridor. His return at 1:35 is now a
recognition rather than an introduction, which is worth far more than the time it costs.

**The apparatus measures the party at its peak, 1:21.** *THE METER IN THE DOORWAY*, three
seconds, on a vibe reading of 77. Nobody in the party reacts to it, which is the point:
they don't know they are being assessed. Three seconds is enough because the audience was
told at 0:10 what exceeding your supply means.

**And the payoff is now a physical act, at 1:58.** Everything about the Softening until
this point is soft — leaflets, water bottles, decibel readings, a smug man with a book.
*THEY CUT THE POWER* is the one moment the argument puts a hand on something: a gloved
hand in a sage-green sleeve pulls a main breaker and the room goes out in the same frame.

It sits on the steepest fall in the telemetry. The number drops 63 → 26 underneath it,
because the room really did go quiet at that point in the recording. This also collapses
two abstract slates into one concrete image: cutting the power and deleting the record are
the same action, since with the room dark the meter has nothing to read and the evidence
ends with the light.

### The field placement, fixed properly

Section 8 shipped with a known fault: the Act IV field shot was rendered from the collapse
at 1:59 and cut in at 2:14, where the chrome reads 0.0. The picture and the number
disagreed, in the one film whose entire claim is that they can't.

The fix was not to move the shot. `tools/lifesim/export_curve.py` now **reads each field
shot's window straight off the EDL** rather than from a hand-typed table, so a shot is
always rendered from the telemetry it will actually play over. Move it in the cut and its
telemetry follows. That table turned out to be wrong for the Act II biota shot as well, by
three seconds, which nobody had noticed.

The retraction now plays at 2:01 on a falling 18, and a second, dimmer field — seeded
apart, pulled back, barely orbiting — was rendered for the flatline at 2:14 where the
reading really is zero. Two shots, each honest about its own number.

### The transformation, which had been cut out entirely

A worse fault than the antagonist one, and harder to see because nothing looked
missing: **Act III skipped the moment the scientists become beasts.** The cut ran
0:30–0:34.5 of the source and then jumped to 0:42, which is the aftermath — a
macro of a shredded coat and a surviving tie. Everything between those two points
was gone, and everything between those two points is the event.

Frame-stepping the source at four frames a second shows how narrow the window is.
The whole thing occupies **39.1 to 42.0**, under three seconds:

| source | |
|---|---|
| 39.1–40.0 | BLOOD MOON IMMINENT — the trigger card |
| 40.4–42.0 | the turn itself |
| 42.0–44.0 | the aftermath macro: coat in ribbons, blue tie intact |

And the turn is 1.6 seconds. It works by alternating frames — a pale, almost
white silhouette, then a dark beast, then pale again — so the figure resolves out
of its own shadow while the lab coat comes apart around it. At source speed that
alternation is below the threshold where you read it as anything; it registers as
a flicker and then there are werewolves.

So it is now held at half speed: 1.6 seconds of source over 3.2 seconds of
screen, which is the only speed change anywhere in the film. Frames are held
rather than interpolated, deliberately — smoothing between them would blur the
pale and dark states into each other and destroy the exact thing being slowed
down. `C()` takes a `speed` argument for this and nothing else uses it.

The trigger card gets 1.2s in front of it and the aftermath macro 2.4s after, so
the sequence now runs cause, event, consequence. The four seconds this costs come
off the shots on either side of it; Act III still totals thirty.

### Prompts on the cards

Every red card now carries its generation prompt in a right-hand column, so the animatic
is self-describing without a document open beside it. The long versions — negatives,
continuity rules, alternates, priority order — are in `docs/generation-prompts.md`.

Generated shots went from six to seven, 33.5 seconds of 180, still under a fifth of the
film. Slate copy was also corrected to quote real telemetry values rather than
approximations; three cards had been quoting numbers the curve does not contain.

### Delivered

Public repo, cut attached as a release, prompts and outline alongside it:
<https://github.com/fractastical/moonshot_the_movie>

## 11. The finality pass — built

The film was retitled **MOONSHOT** and rebuilt around a single idea: the moon has
been filed as done, and a finished world does not need power. Everything below
follows from that, and none of it cost a second of runtime — all five acts still
land on their original score boundaries to the frame.

### The ritual finally has a cause

`THEIR POWER WAS CUT` (0:35.6, 3.5s) now opens Act II: a rack of computers
powering down, nobody in frame. Until this card existed, twenty people sat in a
dome because the story needed them to. Now Act II is a substitution for equipment
they can no longer run, the circle is a transmitter rather than a séance, and the
ZVEN prompt has grown an exterior — the beams have to leave the dome or the
moonshot is not in the film. The dorje gave up 2.5 of its 5 seconds to pay for it.

### The moon was missing from the middle, and the footage was already in hand

The moon appeared three times in Act I, three times in Act V, and not once in
between — it bookended rather than fought. The cut had been jumping from source
51.5 to 72.0 since the first assembly, and sitting in that gap is a grey moon
filling frame and turning blood-orange. `THE MOON ANSWERS` (1:33.7, 1.9s) closes
Act III on it, paid for by a second off the field shot, and it lands four seconds
after *"and still nobody could tell me what it was for."* It also pays off the
`BLOOD MOON IMMINENT` card fired earlier in the same act.

In at 54.05, not 53.9: a scanline glitch sits on the first three frames.

### The machine became the third party

It was two thirds built and nobody had noticed. `THE MACHINE DISAGREES` already
sat on the film's central line — he calls the readout as his witness and it
refuses in the same second. That is the refusal. The commitment is new:
`THE MACHINE PICKS A SIDE` (2:46.4, 4.0s), where the trace doubles and the second
line *leads* the first, the job field flips WATCHING → JOINING IN, and the number
goes past its own scale. The film opens with TOO MUCH as an accusation on a wall
and closes with the same two words as the machine's own reading.

It never speaks. A signature is not an argument, and the script's rule that nothing
rebuts him in words survives intact.

Act V paid for it out of the lunar room, the moon dance and the van portrait. His
last line now lands *inside* that card, so he files an intention about the moon
while the thing commits behind him.

### The title changed hands

The last card is `MOONSHOT`. He named the event a problem; the film names it an
attempt, and the naming is the final move of the conflict rather than a credit.
*Turn down the moon* survives where it does its best work — as his line at 2:47,
and as the operation designation on a document at 1:42. Not destroy. Dim.

### Cost

Eight generated shots now, 37.0 seconds of 180, still barely a fifth of the film.
One new reference plate (`lab`). Two new VO lines and one pickup, numbered 28–30
out of order so that anything already recorded against the old numbering still
matches.

## 12. The amber cards — built

`tools/video/panels.py`. The seven RENDER cards no longer say what should go there;
they play it. 30.3 seconds, 17% of the film, recovered from text to picture without a
camera or a generator — because the film already owned the thing they are shots *of*.

The language is the real operator UI rather than an invention: phosphor green on
near-black, everything tracked out, the tall subject meters down both edges, the VIBE
block with its sparkline and L/M/R split, `ID 0NN` tabs on detection boxes, three-word
pins on the map. Compare `docs/img/observer-vibe.png` and `observer-geomap.png`.

Every number on these panels is read out of `curve.py` at that frame, so they cannot
drift from the chrome, the score or the grade. Two consequences worth stating:

- **They take the vibe grade like any other picture.** The phosphor drains as the
  number drains, for free — the console is nearly grey at `NOTHING LEFT TO DO`
  (vibe 6.0) and full green at `THE MACHINE PICKS A SIDE` (86.7). The title card is
  the one exception: typography, not a screen.
- **The telemetry overruled the copy twice.** `SIGNAL STRENGTH` was written as a climb
  from 20 to 32; the real curve holds near 27 and breaks to 41.1 in the last half
  second, so the card was rewritten to describe a room that catches rather than ramps.
  `THE TURN` was written as a leisurely 0 → 1 → 2; Act V's boundary is a slam — 54 on
  the first sample, 82 by the third — so the tick happens inside the first half second
  and the count then simply tracks the number.

Three cards do work the text could only assert. `NOTHING LEFT TO DO` builds its six
empty monitors out of **real frames from the exhibition's own capture archive**
(`~/Documents/surveille`), six different rooms the rig actually photographed, which is
the claim that card is making. `THE MACHINE DISAGREES` puts eleven detection boxes
across two cameras of a provably full room beside a meter reading 14.5, then stamps
`READING REFUSED — THE MACHINE WILL NOT SIGN THIS` over it. And `THE MACHINE PICKS A
SIDE` plots not the last eight seconds but **the entire record** — the flatline,
both climbs, the erasure, the ten seconds of zero — with a dashed projection running
past a NOW marker and off the top of a scale it has just exceeded at 103.2.

One bug fell out of building them. Truncating each act's sample count independently
left the final sample of the curve unwritten, indistinguishable from a reading of
zero, so the film ended on three frames of `VIBE 0.0` with the grade slammed shut.
Fixed in `curve.py`; the last frame now reads 81.0.

## 13. The plot reads at a fifth-grade level

The premise is strange — the moon was filed as finished, so electricity is rationed —
and the film had been *implying* it rather than saying it. A viewer who did not
already know the story could watch all three minutes without ever learning why the
power was cut. Every carrier of plot was rewritten, and the constraint is now
enforced by `tools/video/reading_level.py`, which reads the spoken lines out of
`docs/vo-script.md` and the on-screen strings out of `panels.py` and fails if either
scores above fifth grade or if two lines collide.

Spoken lines went from grade 2.2 with eighteen hard words to **grade 0.8 with none**;
on-screen words from 7.7 with twenty-five to **2.3 with none**. But the score was the
smaller half of it. Three structural changes did the actual work:

- **The premise is now spoken aloud, once, in A2 at 0:14:** *"We already did the big
  thing. So nobody needs much power."* The film previously said
  "everyone is allocated what a finished world requires", which is the same sentence
  written so that only a policy reader can parse it.
- **Act I was re-ordered so his authorship causes her loss.** He says "I wrote the
  rules" at 0:25 and she answers "They took our money. Last week, our power." at 0:27.
  Previously those two facts sat four seconds apart in the other order and read as
  unrelated. Same two lines, same act total, and the audience now gets cause and
  effect for free.
- **The console stopped talking like a console.** `SUBJECTS IN FRAME` → `PEOPLE RIGHT
  NOW`, `CELEBRATION INDEX` → `PARTY LEVEL`, `INSTRUMENT DECLINES TO CERTIFY` → `THE
  MACHINE WILL NOT SIGN THIS`, `ROLE: OBSERVER` → `MY JOB: WATCHING`. The chrome's act
  names went with them: `IV softening` was only ever legible to us, and is now
  `IV attack`.

Two rewrites turned out to be improvements rather than concessions. The state's phrase
for the condition is now literally *nothing left to do*, so the words on the card at
0:19 are the words B says over it, which the euphemism `NOTHING OUTSTANDING` could
never do. And the opening notice now reads `YOU HAVE USED TOO MUCH POWER`, which lets
the last card put **TOO MUCH** in red as the machine's own reading of them — the
film's opening accusation returned as its verdict, in the two plainest words it owns.

Rewriting cost length: 281 words became 300, which broke the timing in seven places.
The collision checker was written for exactly that, and all thirty-six lines were
re-timed against the picture. 128 seconds of speech, 52 of silence, no overlaps.

## 14. Open questions

- Which generator for the eight shots, and can it hold one consistent look?
- Do we have the ZVEN ritual source, or only what's in the contest cut? The beams
  leaving the dome are now load-bearing, which raises the stakes on this.
- The guard-rail line about whose telemetry gets deleted — where does it sit?
- Does Act I at 35 seconds test as patient or as slow? It is the single riskiest call here.
- `MOONSHOT` collides with a prominent AI lab of the same name, in a film about
  summoning an AI. Artistically clean, findability less so — worth a subtitle if it
  bites at submission.
