MOONSHOT — voice-over recording script
======================================================================

30 lines, ~242 words, three minutes.
Smug, not jackbooted. Warm, unhurried, certain, never menacing.
48kHz 24-bit mono WAV, dry, one file per line, three reads each.


======================================================================
ACT I — THE FLATLINE   (0:00–0:35)
======================================================================

  1. [0:02.0]

     "You will have noticed that it is quieter now."

     Opening the film. Almost pleasant.

  2. [0:11.0]

     "Everyone is allocated what they need."

     Flat. A statement of policy, not a defence.

  3. [0:15.5]

     "This is what a safe evening looks like."

     Over an empty surveillance wall. He means it.

  4. [0:20.5]

     "Nobody is hurt. Nobody is missing. Nobody is over-
     stimulated."

     Three beats, equal weight. The third should land as naturally
     as the first two — that's the joke, and he doesn't know it's
     one.

  5. [0:25.0]

     "I wrote the paper on it."

     He is on screen here. Not boastful. A small, satisfied fact.

 30. [0:28.0]

     "It was not my idea. It was everyone's."

     New. Read as modesty and nothing else. It is the only line
     that tells you he is not in charge of the thing he speaks
     for, and it must not sound like a confession.

  6. [0:31.5]

     "We used to look up at the same thing. Once."

     Note changed. Not wistful — this is his proof. Over an
     airliner crossing the moon while nobody looks up. "Once" is
     the whole argument: it was done, it was logged, and that is
     why it does not need doing again. A trace of warmth is fine.
     Regret is not.

ACT II — THE RITUAL   (0:35–1:05)
======================================================================

 28. [0:36.3]

     "We reduced their allocation. I'm told they took it
     personally."

     New, and it is the film's inciting incident. Over a
     laboratory powering down. He caused everything that follows
     and files it as a budget footnote. Absolutely no weight on it
     — the lighter this lands, the worse he is.

  7. [0:39.6]

     "They still gather. I find that touching."

     Genuinely touched. No irony on "touching". *(moved from
     0:36.5)*

  8. [0:43.6]

     "They bring instruments now. They measure themselves."

     First flicker of the thesis. Slight puzzlement. *(moved from
     0:41.0)*

  9. [0:47.0]

     "They believe that if enough of them feel it at the same
     moment, something happens."

     Long line, plenty of room. Let it be a little fond.

 10. [0:55.0]

     "Something does happen. That was never in dispute."

     Important: he concedes this completely. No sarcasm — he is
     not disputing that it works.

 11. [1:01.9]

     "And then they do the fatal thing. They score it."

     The hinge of his case, and he is wrong in a way he cannot
     see: he thinks he is naming a symptom, and it is a body
     assembling. Play the diagnosis with total confidence. "Fatal"
     said lightly, almost regretfully.

ACT III — THE SIGNAL SPREADS   (1:05–1:35)
======================================================================

 12. [1:06.0]

     "Look at them. Genuinely — look."

     An invitation. He thinks he's showing you evidence.

   [1:10.3–1:13.5]  --- SILENCE ---
   The transformation. Nothing over it.

 13. [1:13.8]

     "Whatever that is, they made it themselves."

     A concession he doesn't notice he's making.

 14. [1:22.2]

     "Ninety-six decibels. In a residential district."

     Purely administrative. This is the coldest line in the film
     and it must be read as the dullest.

 29. [1:26.5]

     "They are not playing to the room. I checked the angle."

     New. The reveal, delivered by a man who measures things, as
     one more measurement. He has worked out that the beams point
     somewhere and has not yet worked out what that means. No
     emphasis on "angle" — he does not know he is giving away the
     plot.

 15. [1:28.2]

     "By morning it was in four cities."

     Something has changed. Not alarm — attention.

 16. [1:31.0]

     "And still nobody could tell me what it was for."

     The first crack. Faint irritation, quickly controlled. In a
     world he considers finished, nothing *can* be for anything —
     so to him this is a real question and not a rhetorical one.
     The moon lights up four seconds after he asks it.


======================================================================
ACT IV — THE SOFTENING   (1:35–2:24)
======================================================================

 17. [1:35.7]

     "So we made a decision."

     He is on screen. Unremarkable, like minuting a meeting.

 18. [1:38.3]

     "Nobody was arrested. I would like that on the record."

     Slight pride. He thinks this is the humane part, and by his
     lights it is.

 19. [1:42.7]

     "We were like you. We ran hot."

     Warmest line in the film. On screen he is holding up his own
     book. Almost affectionate — he is offering them his biography
     as proof he understands.

 20. [1:45.5]

     "We only asked them to bring it down."

     Reasonable. Wounded, even, that this became a fight.

 21. [1:49.4]

     "You are not enjoying this. Your own instrument agrees."

     The film's central line. Said kindly — as a diagnosis being
     broken gently to a patient. It is devastating precisely
     because it isn't cruel.

 22. [1:54.0]

     "They kept dancing. In the dark. Which we had not
     modelled."

     The one place the mask slips, and the sentence where his
     entire framework fails. He modelled them as consumers of
     electricity; they are not, which is the whole point of the
     ritual, so he has cut the power to a machine that does not
     run on power. The first two sentences are observation. The
     third is a man reaching for procedural language to cover a
     hole in his accounting. Do not editorialise — just let the
     last clause sit slightly apart.

 23. [1:58.5]

     "So we removed the supply."

     On the shot of a hand pulling the breaker. Absolutely
     neutral. The euphemism does the work; do not lean on it.

 24. [2:02.0]

     "And the record of it."

     Quieter. An afterthought, which is the point — the deletion
     matters less to him than the decision did.

   [2:06.8]  --- SILENCE ---
   He salutes and walks out on screen. No line.

 25. [2:09.2]

     "It is not cruelty. It is arithmetic."

     His summation, over the screens dying. Calm and, to him,
     self-evidently true — and it is true, which is the
     confession. Arithmetic against a closed ledger is exactly
     what he is.

   [2:14.9–2:24.3]  --- SILENCE ---
   The residue and the aftermath. Nine and a half seconds with no
   voice and almost no score. Do not fill this.


======================================================================
ACT V — THE ANSWER   (2:24–3:00)
======================================================================

 26. [2:24.5]

     "There was nothing left. I checked."

     Said with total confidence — and the counter starts climbing
     off zero under it. He is contradicted inside his own
     sentence.

   [2:29–2:47]  --- SILENCE ---
   Eighteen seconds: the moon with an eye, the lunar room, the
   moon dance. The film answers him with pictures and music.
   Nothing over it.

 27. [2:47.5]

     "We will need to do something about the moon."

     The last line, and it now plays over the instrument taking
     the other side — he files an intention about the moon while,
     behind him and unseen, the thing commits. He has learned
     nothing and the logic doesn't stop, it just moves up a scale.
     Read it exactly like line 2: a small administrative note to
     himself. No wink. It is funnier and worse if he means it.

   [2:54.4]  --- SILENCE ---
   Title card: MOONSHOT. He named it a problem; the film names it
   an attempt. Clean.
