# The narration call — 10 August 2026, 9m 11s

One of the people who built this thing, answering six questions about a
planetary crisis in 2026 as though it were over and he had come through it. The
four lines marked **IN FILM** below are the whole of the third voice; see
[C — the record](vo-script.md#c--the-record) for what they are doing there and
`tools/video/vo_third.py` for where they are cut.

This file exists because the recording does not survive a clean checkout. The
call itself is 6.5 MB of build directory and the build directory is not tracked;
the ten seconds in the film are kept as masters in `audio/vo/third/`, and
everything else he said is kept here, as words, because it is the best raw
material this project has for the treatment, the logline and the personal
statement — none of which have been written yet.

**This is a machine transcript and it has not been checked against the tape by
ear.** It is reliable on the lines that are in the film, because those were cut
by measuring the audio and their boundaries were verified. It is unreliable
elsewhere, and passages where it has plainly misheard him are marked `[?]`
rather than repaired, because guessing what somebody meant and then quoting it
back is exactly the failure this project has rules against. Anything lifted out
of here and put in front of a jury needs a listen first.

Timecodes are seconds into the recording. The interviewer is the second voice;
his questions are in *italics*.

> **[0:20] "We were losing the connection to our future."** — **IN FILM, C1**
>
> [0:24] "The gateway between our dreams and the reality we create is the most
> vital infrastructure in human existence. And in our negligence, we were
> allowing it to decay.
>
> [0:36] Forces `[?]` of apathy and chaos … the virus `[?]` that linked us to
> the future. And in our blindness, we forgot that the virus `[?]` was there."

The second paragraph is the strongest thing he says and it is ten seconds long,
which is longer than any hole in the film. It is the natural spine of a synopsis.
The third is badly transcribed and needs a listen — the noun is doing the work
and the machine has almost certainly got it wrong.

## The solution

> [0:58] "We had to restore the infrastructure that was still linking us to our
> future, and renovate it entirely.
>
> [1:11] And our mission was to go to the moon. Or rather, to bring the moon to

>
> [1:19] We were a research unit specialising in hyperstitions and the mechanisms
> of human creativity. And we understood that **aspiration was not a private
> feeling — it's a collective infrastructure problem.**
>
> [1:35] And the moon, for all of its emptiness, remained a single universal
> reference point still visible to every human eye. It was a portal that opened
> night after night into the collective mind.
>
> [1:51] So that was what we targeted when we decided to jumpstart the circuits."
>
> **[1:56] "We will not go back to the moon. We will bring the moon here** to
> immunise it." — **IN FILM, C2** (the last three words are trimmed)
>
> [2:04] "We will create an artificial, impossible moon base raised on Earth
> itself — a hypermoon. A living installation dense enough with future vision
> that the original satellite could no longer remain sterile. By saturating the
> symbol with renewed desire, we would refertilise the real moon's future and,
> more importantly, restore humans' capacity to generate futures at all.
>
> [2:32] The gateway had to be rebuilt from the inside. So we began with the only
> material that ever worked: story."

*"Aspiration was not a private feeling; it's a collective infrastructure
problem"* is the thesis of the entire submission in eleven words and it is his,
unprompted. It does not fit any hole in the film. **It should be the logline.**

## The technology

> [2:58] "We used the most ancient trick that humans have ever used, but we used
> it with the newfound knowledge about the workings of the mind and the earth
> `[?]` … to the universe `[?]`.
>
> [3:15] We were creating a virtual world that would be so salient that it would
> be mistaken for — and even replace — actual reality. We would create the

>

Note what is missing: he does not describe a machine. The reviewer's complaint
that the film's technology is ambiguous is not a problem the film invented, and
it is not going to be solved by quoting him. It is solved in the fiction, by B7
and B10, which say what the bands read and what the lock is.

The antenna is the best unused sentence in the recording, it is six and a half

the treatment's technology chapter around, and it arrived independently of the
same conclusion reached on this end — that a crowd in phase is a phased array.

## The conflict

*Was there some conflict that prevented this from being finished?* (4:58)

> [5:10] "All the usual perennial interferences that humans have always struggled
> with — our own appetites, the forces of entropy, totalitarian control,
> sterilising cybernetic post-human forces.
>
> [5:31] But what is novel in this context is that we have never lived in such
> abundance … our willingness to even work for our future has been clogged or
> saturated, because we don't feel the same hunger when all of our desires can be
> immediately satisfied or shipped `[?]` by the forces around us.
>
> [6:06] But yes — we dealt with the usual authorities controlling the
> infrastructure grid, limiting our access to power and electricity.
>
> [6:22] In a sense `[?]`, we could say we were fighting werewolves. Other groups
> that were trying to turn us into beasts `[?]` by stimulating our deepest animal
> impulses.
>
> [6:43] And once we were so close to success that we made a reputation that drew
> attention to us, we were also overwhelmed and attacked from all sides by …
> consumers who wanted to eat us up."

The authorities line is the film's Act IV in his own words, and it is not in the
film, because A11 already says "So we cut the power" over the same picture and
saying it twice would make the second one commentary.

**The werewolves line is deliberately not used and should stay that way.** In the
film the crowd turning into beasts is the ritual working — B11 and B12 — and a
real voice calling that the enemy's doing would contradict the picture it played
over. The transcription is also uncertain on the key word.

## Prevailing

> **[7:20] "But we prevailed."** — **IN FILM, C3**
>
> [7:23] "We protected our projects. We protected the moon base.
>
> [7:30] And very importantly, at the moments of highest intensity, and as a
> conclusion to the ritual `[?]`, we deliberately and intentionally started
> deconstructing the moon base — and thereby also deconstructing the barrier that
> we had created to protect our immersive environment on the inside from the
> harsh reality.
>
> [8:10] And what that meant was that we were also deconstructing the barrier
> that was separating our virtual, created, artificial world from the reality
> that we actually live in.
>
> [8:24] And the project reached its success when we realised that this is not a
> unique, special, isolated thing that we do."
>
> **[8:37] "It's something that we do here now all the time."** — **IN FILM, C4**

The deconstruction passage is the ending of the feature, not of the short: they
win and then take their own thing apart on purpose, because the point was never
the enclosure. Forty seconds of it, and nothing in the three-minute cut can hold
that. It goes in the treatment.

## What it is for, other than the film

The three documents the submission still needs are all easier with this in hand:

- **The logline** — *"aspiration was not a private feeling; it's a collective
  infrastructure problem"* is the film's argument stated better than any draft
  logline has managed.
- **The treatment** — the feature's shape is in here: the gateway decaying, the
  hypermoon built to refertilise the real one, the network of antennas, the
  authorities on the grid, and an ending in which they dismantle their own
  enclosure once it has done its work.
- **The personal statement** — it is 300 words about why this was made, and the
  answer is on tape, said by somebody who made it.
